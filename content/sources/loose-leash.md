# Loose-lead walking

- [Teaching your dog to walk on a loose leash](https://www.maddiesfund.org/teaching-your-dog-to-walk-on-a-loose-leash.htm) — Maddie's Fund
- [How to stop your dog pulling the lead](https://www.rspcaqld.org.au/resources/pet-care/how-to-stop-your-dog-pulling-the-lead-MCDU4OMEJLXZBIZEIZ7P3N2PSFOQ) — RSPCA Queensland
- Cross-check: Ontario SPCA, Purina and Rover lead-training guides

Common ground:

- Get the puppy comfortable with the collar or harness and lead first, with treats.
- Reward often for being next to you while the lead is slack, every few steps at first.
- When the lead goes tight, stop and wait (or turn and go the other way); carry on once it's slack.
- Start indoors or in the garden, then quiet streets, then busier places.
- Equipment: a flat collar or a front-attaching harness; no extending leads for training.
- Reward the slack lead rather than punishing the tight one.

## Stop-go on walks (S20.1, 25 Nov 2026)

- [How to train your dog to walk on a lead](https://www.battersea.org.uk/pet-advice/dog-advice/how-teach-your-dog-walk-lead) — Battersea
- [How to stop a dog pulling on the lead (step by step)](https://ask.woodgreen.org.uk/pet-advice/dog/behaviour-and-training/how-to-stop-your-dog-pulling-on-the-lead-step-by-step) — Woodgreen
- RSPCA Queensland (above); cross-check: Dogs Trust's page on pulling, which explains why stepping forward when the dog pulls teaches that pulling works

Common ground:

- When the lead tightens, pause and wait for the dog to come back or the tension to ease, then move on; reward each moment the lead is slack, at your leg.
- If waiting doesn't work, reset: turn the other way, or start again from a couple of steps.
- Everyone who walks the dog uses the same rules; consistency matters more than any piece of equipment.
- A lead of about 1.5–2 m, no extending or slip leads; a fitted harness if the dog pulls on a collar.
- No telling off and no pulling the dog back with the lead.
