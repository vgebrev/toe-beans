# Settling

- [My dog won't settle: teaching your dog to be calm and relaxed](https://www.battersea.org.uk/pet-advice/dog-advice/my-dog-wont-settle-teaching-your-dog-be-calm-and-relaxed) — Battersea
- [Teaching Calm: SOFT and Handling Exercises](https://vcahospitals.com/know-your-pet/dog-behavior-and-training---teaching-calm---soft-and-handling-exercises) — VCA Animal Hospitals
- Cross-check: Dr Karen Overall's Relaxation Protocol (as described by trainers)

Common ground:

- A portable mat used only for this; drop treats on the mat so it becomes a good place.
- Reward small steps: interest in the mat, paws on it, lying down, then signs of relaxing (hip rolled, chin down).
- Build duration slowly with calm, low-key rewards; keep your own energy low.
- Over-tired puppies struggle to settle: they need a lot of sleep.
- No scolding; if they wander off, wait and reward the return.

## Car rides, calmly (S20.2, 25 Nov 2026)

- [Car sickness and motion sickness in dogs](https://www.pdsa.org.uk/pet-help-and-advice/pet-health-hub/conditions/car-sickness-and-motion-sickness-in-dogs) — PDSA (vet charity)
- [Carsickness in Dogs: What to Know](https://www.akc.org/expert-advice/training/car-sickness-in-dogs/) — American Kennel Club

Common ground:

- A ladder of small steps: near the parked car, in it with doors open and engine off, doors closed, engine on, a minute or two of driving, then short trips building a few minutes at a time; each step practised over a few days, back a step at any sign of worry.
- Signs of sickness (drooling, lip licking, swallowing, retching) and of fear (whining, shaking, pulling away from the car) overlap. Motion sickness is common in puppies, whose inner ear is still developing.
- Restraint: a secured crate or travel harness; the AKC suggests the middle of the back seat. Cool air reaching the dog; windows open a little.
- Feeding differs: the PDSA says feed at least 2–3 hours before; the AKC suggests much longer without food. We follow the PDSA, the vet charity, and the backlog's "a light meal hours before".
- Medication only through a vet; the PDSA says human and non-prescription remedies are often ineffective. We recommend none (NFR-14).

## A first Stay (S20.3, 25 Nov 2026)

- [How to Teach Your Dog to Stay](https://www.akc.org/expert-advice/training/teaching-the-stay/) — American Kennel Club
- [Stay 1: Duration](https://www.oregonhumane.org/portland-training/stay-1-duration/) — Oregon Humane Society
- Cross-check: Purina UK's guide to "stay" and "wait" (sponsored, so not cited)

Common ground:

- Teach the release word first; the reward is for staying, so it's given in position, before the release.
- Duration first, then distance, then distraction, one at a time, making the others easier while a new one is added. Always go back to the dog rather than calling them out.
- Puppies: short stays, little or no distance at first, short sessions spread through the day.
- If the dog breaks, no reward and no fuss; ask again and make it easier. The AKC says not to punish; Oregon Humane uses a mild "too bad" marker, which we leave out.
