# Doorbell sounds

- [How to prepare your dog for visitors](https://www.dogstrust.org.uk/dog-advice/training/home/visitor-training) — Dogs Trust
- [Creative solutions to poor door greeting behavior](https://www.oregonhumane.org/portland-training/creative-solutions-to-poor-door-greeting-behavior/) — Oregon Humane Society
- [Desensitization and counter-conditioning](https://eastbayspca.org/wp-content/uploads/2024/05/Desensitization-and-Counter-Conditioning-Canine.pdf) — East Bay SPCA

Common ground:

- Manage real visitors while training: a note asking people not to knock or ring.
- Start with the sound quiet (a recording, or a gentle knock on a wall) and follow every sound with a treat, so it predicts good things.
- Raise the volume and realism only when the puppy stays relaxed.
- Then add a go-to-bed (or mat) behaviour for when the bell rings; Dogs Trust suggests short sessions of about 10 minutes.
- Starting early with puppies stops the bell becoming exciting at all.
